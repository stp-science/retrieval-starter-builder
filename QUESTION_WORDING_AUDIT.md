# Question wording audit — 13 October 2026

## Coverage and method

Checked the 10,192 active main-bank and One Worders entries across 171 topics, covering Years 7–13 and IB. The audit uses the assembled, clarified prompts displayed by the builder, rather than only the original source text. All entries were checked programmatically for wording, standalone context, grammar, punctuation, prompt length and stable processing. Flagged constructions and generated-question patterns were reviewed and corrected; this is not a claim that every entry received an independent subject-specialist review.

The changes improve 568 displayed question entries (418 distinct original wordings) across 118 topics. Seven answer entries were corrected or expanded to match the requested response. Topic selection, curriculum scope and difficulty settings are retained. Description and explanation questions receive the appropriate response type.

## Changes

- Repaired incomplete commands, misplaced auxiliary verbs and broken relative clauses.
- Replaced truncated IB significance questions with explicit scientific tasks and named the relevant process, quantity or system.
- Replaced hidden references to features “stated in” a topic with self-contained comparisons.
- Used “State whether … Explain your answer” for misconception questions, so students know both parts of the response are required.
- Replaced selected generic scientific-skills prompts with specific tasks, and shortened the remaining investigation wording.
- Clarified smallest-to-largest ordering, named scientists in the requested order, and corrected percentage-of-air grammar.
- Defined acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, separated blood's transport of substances from its transfer of thermal energy, and aligned several other answers with the new prompts.

## Examples

| Previous wording | Revised wording |
| --- | --- |
| State reached when particles are evenly distributed. | Name the state reached when diffusing particles are evenly distributed. |
| Explain whether a hard can material also be brittle. | State whether a material can be both hard and brittle. Explain your answer. |
| State the approximate percentage of dry air is nitrogen. | State the approximate percentage of nitrogen in dry air. |
| Explain why orbital speed. | Explain how orbital speed can be determined by equating gravitational force and centripetal force. |
| Describe the two contrasting features of refraction stated in Wave phenomena. | Describe what happens to frequency, speed and wavelength when a wave is refracted into a medium with a different wave speed. |
| Explain why “systematic error” matters when carrying out or evaluating a scientific investigation. | Explain why repeating readings cannot remove a systematic error. |
